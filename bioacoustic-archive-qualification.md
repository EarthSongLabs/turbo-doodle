# Bioacoustic Archive Qualification and Source Comparison

**Domain / Role:** Acoustic ecology / Acoustic ecologist  
**Goal:** Identify and compare publicly accessible bioacoustic archives containing scientifically credible recordings that can be legally reused for ecological research and musical investigation.

**Applications:** Firefox; Microsoft Excel  
**Deliverable:** An Excel source qualification matrix with traceable evidence, documented uncertainties, and application-specific recommendations.

## Step 1 — Identify and evaluate candidate archives

**Application: Firefox**

1. Search for publicly accessible bioacoustic archives, including marine and terrestrial acoustic datasets.
2. Review each archive's documentation, collection scope, access procedures, and licensing terms.
3. Examine individual recording pages or metadata records to determine whether archive-level claims apply to specific recordings.
4. Evaluate the following criteria:
   - **Licensing:** Public domain, CC0, Creative Commons, or other applicable permissions and restrictions.
   - **Species coverage:** Taxonomic diversity, geographic distribution, and ecological relevance.
   - **Recording specifications:** Documented sample rate, bit depth, channel configuration, recording duration, and file format.
   - **Scientific provenance:** Collection institution, recording methods, equipment, location, date, and associated research.
   - **Metadata completeness:** Species identification, environmental context, recording conditions, and annotations.
   - **Accessibility:** Download availability, supported formats, access restrictions, and technical requirements.
5. Record direct links to supporting archive documentation and representative recording records.

**Verification:** Distinguish archive-wide policies from recording-specific permissions and metadata. Mark information as unverified when supporting evidence is unavailable.

## Step 2 — Build the archive qualification matrix

**Application: Microsoft Excel**

Create a workbook with one primary comparison row per archive and supporting recording-level evidence.

Suggested columns:

| Category | Fields |
|---|---|
| Archive identity | Archive name, organization, URL |
| Coverage | Species/taxa, geographic regions, habitats |
| Licensing | License, commercial reuse, modification rights, attribution requirements |
| Technical specifications | Sample rate, bit depth, channels, file formats |
| Provenance | Recording date/location, equipment, collection methods |
| Metadata | Species identification, annotations, environmental context |
| Access | Download method, restrictions, registration requirements |
| Research suitability | Supported ecological analyses and limitations |
| Musical suitability | Potential for pitch analysis, synthesis, sampling, or composition |
| Evidence | Documentation URLs, recording-level examples |
| Uncertainties | Missing information, unresolved licensing or technical questions |
| Recommendation | Suitability assessment and justification |

Use separate worksheets for:

- **Archive Comparison:** Summary assessment of each source.
- **Recording Evidence:** Representative recordings, direct links, documented specifications, and licensing evidence.
- **Recommendations:** Application-specific conclusions and outstanding verification requirements.

Keep documented technical specifications separate from subjective or personally assessed audio quality. Do not assign listening-based quality ratings unless the recordings have actually been examined.

## Step 3 — Verify evidence and develop recommendations

**Applications: Firefox and Microsoft Excel**

1. Revisit each archive's primary documentation and representative recording records.
2. Verify licensing statements against the applicable terms for the specific recordings.
3. Check the accuracy of technical specifications, species coverage, metadata, and download methods.
4. Identify any unsupported assumptions, inconsistent documentation, or missing evidence.
5. Evaluate archive suitability separately for:
   - **Ecological research:** Reproducibility, provenance, metadata completeness, scientific reliability, and suitability for quantitative analysis.
   - **Musical investigation:** Legal reuse, access to original audio, documented recording characteristics, and potential for creative transformation.
6. Record recommendations with explicit evidence links, limitations, and unresolved questions.

Avoid treating publicly accessible recordings as automatically licensed for redistribution, modification, or commercial musical use.

## Final deliverable

An Excel workbook containing:

1. A comparative bioacoustic archive qualification matrix.
2. Recording-level evidence and direct source links.
3. Separate recommendations for ecological research and musical investigation.
4. Documented limitations, uncertainties, and verification status.

## Quality-control checklist

- [ ] Every archive has an identifiable primary source.
- [ ] Licensing claims are supported by applicable documentation.
- [ ] Recording-level evidence is distinguished from archive-wide claims.
- [ ] Documented audio specifications are not presented as personally assessed audio quality.
- [ ] Missing metadata and unresolved uncertainties are explicitly identified.
- [ ] Recommendations are traceable to the evidence.
- [ ] Research and musical reuse are evaluated separately.
- [ ] All referenced links and matrix entries have been rechecked.
