# Bioacoustic Archive Qualification and Source Comparison

## Purpose

Evaluate and compare bioacoustic data sources for scientific credibility, technical suitability, accessibility, and lawful reuse in ecological research and creative audio applications.

## Assessment Criteria

| Criterion | Key considerations |
|---|---|
| Licensing | Recording-specific permissions, attribution, modification, redistribution, and commercial use |
| Coverage | Taxa, habitats, geographic distribution, temporal coverage |
| Technical characteristics | Sample rate, bit depth, channels, format, duration, and available original recordings |
| Scientific provenance | Collection methods, equipment, institution, location, date, and associated research |
| Metadata | Identification confidence, annotations, environmental conditions, and completeness |
| Accessibility | Download methods, formats, registration requirements, and restrictions |
| Research suitability | Measurement reliability, reproducibility, representativeness, and analytical limitations |
| Creative suitability | Permitted reuse, signal characteristics, sampling, synthesis, and transformation potential |

## Method

### 1. Identify candidate sources

Locate relevant archives and datasets. Review primary documentation, collection scope, access procedures, and applicable licensing.

### 2. Verify recording-level evidence

Examine representative recording records to establish whether archive-wide statements apply to individual files.

Record supporting URLs, documented technical specifications, provenance, and unresolved questions.

### 3. Compare sources

Maintain a structured comparison with separate records for:

- **Archive Comparison:** Source-level characteristics and suitability.
- **Recording Evidence:** Individual recording metadata, specifications, and permissions.
- **Recommendations:** Application-specific conclusions and outstanding verification requirements.

### 4. Assess suitability

Evaluate each source against the intended analytical or creative application.

Distinguish documented technical specifications from independently measured or perceived audio quality.

Assess scientific and creative suitability separately; a recording appropriate for one purpose may be unsuitable for another.

### 5. Verify conclusions

Recheck primary evidence, identify unsupported assumptions, and document uncertainties.

Assign recommendations only where the available evidence supports them.

## Quality Control

- Verify permissions for the actual recordings under consideration.
- Do not equate public accessibility with public-domain status.
- Preserve traceable links to source documentation.
- Distinguish documented specifications from independently assessed quality.
- Identify missing metadata and unresolved licensing questions.
- Keep scientific and creative suitability assessments separate.
- Reassess recommendations when new evidence becomes available.

## Output

A structured, revisable source qualification matrix with recording-level evidence, documented uncertainty, and application-specific recommendations.
